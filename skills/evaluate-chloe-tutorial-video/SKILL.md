# Evaluate a Chloe tutorial

Ask for or locate the learning objective, givens, intended age, approved reference and playable cut. Start with the learner’s version. Withhold the answer key, source code, author’s explanation and expected verdict during the first viewing. For a comparison, use neutral clip names. Request the actual export or a working player if only a storyboard is available. Clearly scope an excerpt review to that excerpt.

## Verify the learning material

Use the [content and transcript review](references/content-and-transcript.md) for every section. Establish the cohort from the official sources, account for every requested knowledge point, and independently solve or assess all worked and fresh tasks. Log each material claim, key, diagram and model response against its evidence. Keep exam alignment distinct from the quality of an original teaching example.

## First viewing

Play the central example at its normal speed and intended display size. Mute it and hide narration captions for this diagnostic. Keep legitimate evidence such as a passage, axes, numbers, object labels and stated rules. At the prediction pause record what the child has enough information to decide. At the reveal, write down the inference and the specific moment that establishes it. If the interface only exposes stills, inspect chronological samples, name the sampling interval, and mark real-time craft such as pacing as unverified.

Can a learner follow the same object, quantity, wording or condition through the decisive change? A changing quantity should visibly move, split, combine or otherwise account for both sides. A counterexample should keep the relevant givens fixed while the outcome changes. In a reading or argument question a quiet, labelled comparison may carry all the evidence. Distinguish evidence the learner can inspect from prose that announces the result.

## Second viewing

Play with audio and captions. Check whether the spoken cue draws attention to the event as it happens, the learner has time to predict, and the answer stays hidden until the intended reveal. Compare framing, composition, continuity, legibility, sound and pacing with the approved reference. A different subject can use different visual techniques. Check the worked answer independently from the givens, then attempt the fresh question without the key. Compare the approved script with an independent transcription of the final recording, the visible captions and the learner-facing transcript. Account for every narrated beat and resolve meaning-changing differences. Keep the final media identity and timestamps in the review. Use the creator’s encoding and layout checks as additional evidence.

## Verdict and repair

For each objective record **meets**, **revise** or **insufficient evidence**, with a timestamp and what the viewer could actually observe. Report correctness, requested content coverage, exam alignment, spoken words, captions and transcript, and visual reasoning separately. A missing or ambiguous result remains unverified. A correct file and a polished still cannot clear a missing central demonstration. Hold a lesson for revision if its inference depends entirely on reading the worked answer, if the viewer loses an essential object or condition, or if the prediction leaks. Name the smallest shot change that would correct the problem. Re-evaluate the resulting cut at the same timestamps and in full context.

For course release, apply this review to the decisive sequence of *every* lesson. Include a full sound-on, normal-speed viewing before approving a whole lesson. Keep a visible per-lesson result. Disclose a missing listening test, prediction interval or transfer question as unverified. A reviewer’s inference is evidence about the explanation; whether an actual pupil can transfer the skill needs a fresh attempt by that pupil.
