# Producing a course from a textbook

Use this only for a request spanning multiple lessons or sections.

## Coverage and correctness

Inventory the source sections before authoring. Keep a mapping from each source model to its video, worked example, fresh practice, and later review question. Treat the book's labels as its teaching taxonomy unless an official source establishes otherwise. Check current exam dates and format against the relevant official authority.

Read tables and diagrams as well as prose. Solve the examples independently. Record repairs to ambiguous rules, missing assumptions, incorrect arithmetic, and conflicting answer keys. For reasoning problems, preserve exact quantifiers, inclusive/exclusive alternatives, exhaustive route assumptions, position/facing conventions, interval boundaries, and the order of operations.

For Writing, review each prompt, model and fresh response against its intended audience, purpose and task, allowing more than one valid approach. For a large question bank, separate authoring from a fresh solution pass. For selected-response items, verify every option in the section’s format: one intended best answer, meaningful distractors, and enough information in the stem or displayed diagram. Enumeration is useful for finite arrangements, truth tables, cube orientations, and minimum-cost combinations.

## Keep the explanation visual

Reuse the visual language, not just its text boxes. Author a mechanism for the concept: a moving state, disappearing candidate, geometric transformation, cumulative quantity, or tested counterexample. A shared shell is useful, but the worked proof should make the relevant relationship visible. Inspect full-size frames where labels are dense. Keep the complete question and all needed givens visible during a quiet prediction hold after captions disappear. Use arrows for relationships the evidence actually supports; explicitly label proposed causes and uncertain alternatives. Geometric boards need square cells when shape matters, and states need direct object labels.

A hook must connect to a question the video itself resolves, including any in-video practice. A scenario that exists only in a separate website quiz leaves the standalone video without its promised payoff. Do not disclose a later answer in the hook, prediction caption, colour, or preloaded visual. A short quiet recap is acceptable when it is intentionally a reading/reflection scene; missing speech is not.

## Voice consistency and provenance

Use the user's latest voice choice across the course. Reuse a clip only when its spoken text, voice, and generation settings match. Filenames alone do not establish this: keep a content hash or equivalent provenance. Regenerate corrected narration and derive durations and captions again. A final build should fail on missing, stale, or wrong-voice media rather than silently estimating its duration.

## Review each lesson

Keep a review ledger by lesson ID. Inspect the opening, worked mechanism, prediction period, reveal boundary, and recap for every requested lesson. Contact sheets help coverage; open the original frame whenever text or geometry cannot be judged at that scale. Inspect motion at multiple frames when the operation itself carries the explanation. Re-render affected lessons after corrections.

Measure and decode every narration file. Check it fits its scene with a lead and tail and that caption intervals remain in range. Distinguish these technical checks from listening; do not report an audition that was not performed.

### Three independent checks for a narrated course

1. **Solve from the learner’s view.** Give a second reviewer just the stem, visible diagram and answer choices. Record their answer and one reason before showing the key, feedback or textbook solution. Try removing the teaching rule from a transfer question. If the same answer follows directly from a given, rewrite the question so the learner must use the relationship. Check conditional direction, missing cases, units, time boundaries and what a drawing actually shows.
2. **Read the encoded voice back.** Where available, transcribe the final playback files with a speech recogniser. Compare against the authorised script. Review names and local spellings in context. Check every disagreement involving negation, a quantity, a letter label, a unit or the direction of a rule. Listen to the disputed phrase or use a second independent recognition pass. Make the speech unambiguous where necessary. For example, say “turn the first and last cards a quarter turn” when adjacent position numbers and angles run together. Keep the exact written maths on screen. Match the complete caption text to the script and align phrase starts to the measured recording where possible. Record the media hash so a replacement invalidates its previous review.
3. **Inspect the final cut.** For each lesson inspect the opening, a moving proof at two or more frames, the silent prediction frame and the first answer frame. Read the actual on-screen geometry. For a cube, write down the faces visible from that camera. For a chart, read the labelled unit and denominator. Exercise the course player on a remote-sized screen. A corrected clip can move later chapters, pause points and saved playback positions. Retest them with the new timings. Keep worked-proof hints behind submission when they disclose a practice answer.

Record each verdict by lesson and clip with the correction or evidence that resolved it. State which portions received continuous listening or playback and which received machine transcription or frame sampling.

## Learning website

For a requested course site, separate watching, practice, and delayed retrieval. Watching a video is not evidence of mastery. Record whether a question was attempted before its solution, whether reasoning was explained, and whether a review was delayed. Describe self-reported explain-back checks honestly; a checkbox does not grade a proof. Offer official or genuinely unseen mixed practice as the next benchmark.

Preserve old sites and course files. Use a new hosting identity when a new site is requested. Keep public assets self-contained. Browser-local progress should say where it is stored; an export/import backup can support another computer without a personal-data backend. Verify playback, question feedback, persistence, navigation, responsive layout, and the actual hosted assets before handoff.
